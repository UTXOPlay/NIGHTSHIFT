# NIGHTSHIFT 7.0 — World Circuit

World Circuit addresses three concrete problems in Crew Circuit: map cards had faint generic backgrounds, all events reused permutations of four districts, and Street rivals had a sustained speed advantage over the starter car.

## Courses and navigation

Each of the twelve events now owns a distinct environment, landmark set, lighting palette and signed 32-section bend sequence. The selection screen renders the selected course behind the interface. Every event button has a 640×360 WebP capture of that actual course plus its route silhouette; all twelve thumbnails together occupy about 450 KiB. Captures are generated by `tools/nightshift7-map-captures.mjs`, not invented promotional art.

The HUD minimap integrates the same course curvature used by driving. It shows five rival markers, player direction, traversed road and finish. It follows the local road by default; tapping it toggles the full course. Route points are cached once; marker updates run with the 10 Hz HUD. The minimap stays inside the viewport and clear of the timer, speed display and touch controls.

## Rival balance

V6 Street rivals cruised at 1900–2100 units, before boost, against the stock Vanta's 1660. V7 uses a spread of 1520–1800, smaller and shorter boost bursts, and stronger corner braking. The starting grid now spans 6–38 m ahead rather than 30–126 m. Rivals have longer initial attack cooldowns and clearer gaps between attacks. There is no teleporting or hidden catch-up speed adjustment.

A deterministic ordinary-input test reaches third place after one minute in a stock Vanta without activating nitrous, weapons or upgrades. It can still collect the visible road boost strips. The same test reaches fourth on Pro and sixth on Elite. Clean driving with deliberate boosts can reach the lead. The five drivers retain their lane planning, overtaking, defenses and counterable attacks.

All twelve successful races still take 3–7 active minutes. Six cars, earned Relay Credits, three upgrade tracks per car, three weapon choices, signed career persistence, separate intermission screens and mobile thumb-stick controls remain from Crew Circuit.

## Art and rendering

Built-in imagegen produced three new atlases: four biome panoramas, four transparent vegetation cutouts, and four terrain materials. The saved paths and exact prompts are in `nightshift-world-art.json`. V6's VFX and garage atlas remain in use, documented in `nightshift-crew-art.json`.

Foliage uses instanced crossed planes with alpha testing; terrain and landmarks use bounded instance pools. Terrain atlas sampling supplies pre-wrap derivatives and mirrored UVs to avoid tile-edge mip artifacts. Dry maps suppress road reflection sprites and use rougher road materials. Outdoor natural routes omit city streetlights. The renderer keeps the 3,686,400 backing-pixel cap, adaptive resolution, reduced-motion support and cached geometry. No shadow maps or extra full-screen postprocessing passes were introduced.

## Replay and scope

V7 has a new native executable, application profile and data namespace because pace and curvature change game rules. V6 exports remain bound to V6 and are rejected by V7; V7 exports are likewise rejected by V6. Previous careers are retained with their old executable, not silently reinterpreted. The new version starts a separate career.

This is a solo racing career. Live multiplayer still requires an authoritative match clock, authenticated actor slots, networking/reconciliation, lobbies and exactly-once match payouts. Myelin provides off-chain host replay, not CKB settlement. The fictional Nervos relay setting does not imply on-chain currency or transactions.

Original racing references and the prior economy/combat design are recorded in `nightshift-crew-circuit.md`.

## Combat presentation

Four additional generated transparent atlases replace the old rectangular rival markers, filled pickup labels and control/result panel backgrounds. Rival chevrons, target reticles, shield markers and disabled rings communicate different states without a backplate. Pickup silhouettes have distinct colors and shapes. The minimap, results and controls use an open-corner frame with a transparent center; car thumbnails also have transparent backgrounds. Prompts and final asset paths are recorded in `nightshift-combat-art.json`.

Collision, shield depletion, incoming disruption, jammer activation, successful counter, outgoing hit, blocked shot and rival disable each trigger separate feedback. The previous collision flash listener missed `bounce` events; the new transition mapping handles collision and weapon damage explicitly. Incoming projectiles launch in the last six telegraph ticks. Generated sparks, smoke, electrical arcs and shield fragments use a fixed pool of 24 sprites with soft circular edge masks, and shader programs are warmed during loading.

A fatal unshielded crash freezes the authoritative race immediately. Its already-ended result is saved while a 1.7-second visual sequence pitches, rolls and skids the car, with debris and smoke. The results screen then appears with the car resting overturned. A disable makes a rival fishtail and smoke while its existing native seven-second pace penalty runs. These animations do not change collision rules, input mapping or rewards. Reduced motion suppresses transient sprites and rollover and retains a quiet static defense indicator.

`tools/nightshift7-combat-review.mjs` finds the relevant transitions using ordinary input tapes, replays each prefix through the native host, and uses real browser keyboard events for the visible action. It checks collision, incoming hit, successful defense, outgoing hit, disable, rollover, frozen death state, results transition, reduced motion and signed replay. The recorded WebM is an actual browser capture.
